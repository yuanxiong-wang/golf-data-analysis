## Core Takeaway

GKZID should not try to go viral as a generic "all sports" platform first. The better wedge is to attach GKZID cards to moments where athletes already want recognition: leaderboards, tournament finishes, team rosters, milestone posts, and recruiting identity.

Junior golf is a strong first test segment because the Golfstat data gives a real map of athletes, teams, event families, and timing. Use the data for targeting and content planning, not for direct unsolicited outreach to minors.

## Best Initial Wedges

### 1. Leaderboard To Player Card

Message:

> You made the board. Now build the card that tells the story.

Use current Golfstat-style leaderboard moments as content prompts. The call to action should not be "join another platform." It should be "turn your performance into a shareable athlete identity."

### 2. Team Roster Card Packs

Target:

- WIAA high-school teams
- Toyota Junior World Cup teams
- Coach and parent communities

Offer:

- One shareable team page
- Individual player cards
- Team badge or VAN pathway
- Simple copy for coaches and parents to share

### 3. Parent Share Loop

Parents are likely stronger distributors than athletes at the start, especially for junior sports. Build prompts around pride and milestone sharing:

- "First tournament card"
- "State championship card"
- "Top-10 finish card"
- "Season recap card"

### 4. VAN As Trust Layer

VAN should be framed as recognition, not just a badge. The viral loop improves if the athlete can say:

- I built my card
- I shared my story
- I earned recognition
- My team/coach/community can verify me

## Targeting Guardrails

- Do not scrape private contact information.
- Do not mass-message minors.
- Use public leaderboard data for aggregate targeting, examples, timing, and content themes.
- Push opt-in flows through athletes, parents, coaches, teams, and event communities.
- Get permission before featuring real athlete cards in GKZID marketing.
